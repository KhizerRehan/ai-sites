# Flora Radio — creative brief

Create a singular, responsive concept website for **Flora Radio**, a fictional independent listening club where music grows wild. The experience should feel like tuning a late-1970s FM radio inside a lush psychedelic greenhouse: equal parts independent music magazine, vintage broadcast equipment, and botanical collage. It must look handmade, energetic and editorial, with no generic SaaS cards or repetitive template sections.

## Visual direction

Use saturated tomato-coral, electric chartreuse, deep forest ink, warm paper white, and a dash of lilac. Mix giant expressive serif display type, condensed uppercase utility labels, hand-drawn starbursts, offset outlines, paper grain, curved shapes, and a confident asymmetrical editorial grid. The hero must make a memorable visual statement: a colossal FLORA wordmark, an analog radio dial, and a central botanical artwork with a retro radio and oversized flowers. Make typography and art overlap intentionally, while keeping text legible. Use original or generated artwork and avoid cliché generic imagery.

## Experience

1. A full-screen hero introduces the fictional station and its point of view: “Good things grow on air.” Include a clearly labeled interactive analog dial for choosing one of four imaginary shows. Changing the selection updates the dial, show title, description, and a tracklist. Do not imply real audio is streaming; explicitly explain that the dial is an interactive visual concept with no audio.
2. A colorful scrolling ticker bridges the hero and programming.
3. A “Today in the greenhouse” schedule lists four fictional shows, presenters, times and descriptions. Selecting a row updates the highlighted show in the dial. Indicate the schedule is illustrative rather than live.
4. A “Notes from the garden” editorial section explains the station's imagined listening philosophy and showcases a distinctive pull quote.
5. A clearly labeled demo newsletter form confirms locally that it is only a concept, with no submission to a service.
6. A footer includes navigation, fictional concept attribution and a return-to-top link.

## Quality and constraints

- Build an accessible responsive experience that is visually distinctive on desktop and mobile.
- Use semantic elements, usable keyboard controls, clear focus states and sufficient text contrast. Respect reduced motion.
- Keep interactions purposeful and working; do not claim live radio, real performers, ticketing or real subscription services.
- Optimize artwork for the web, test the dial and schedule interactions, and verify responsive layout where preview infrastructure permits.
- Polish every section with original composition and meaningful copy; this should feel like a complete creative brand, not merely a hero mockup.
